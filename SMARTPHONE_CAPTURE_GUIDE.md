## Welcome to the VERIFYX Calibration Capture

This guide explains step-by-step how to take **50 high-quality photos using your smartphone** to complete the VERIFYX safety dataset. 

VERIFYX uses AI on your phone to verify workplace safety. To make sure the AI works reliably in the real world, we need real smartphone photos taken in actual indoor environments—not studio stock photos or artificial web renders.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Safety First — Do NOT Touch or Move Safety Equipment!**  
> Never remove, tamper with, or unhook a fire extinguisher to take a photo. Take photos of equipment exactly as it exists in its real working environment.

## 2. Simple Capture Instructions

Follow these 4 simple steps on your phone:

1. **Camera Settings:**
   - Use your phone's default Camera app.
   - Use **1x standard lens** (do NOT use 0.5x ultra-wide or 3x digital zoom).
   - Use standard **4:3 or 16:9 photo ratio** (do not use square 1:1 or panorama).
   - Turn **Flash OFF** (rely on ambient room lighting).
   - Make sure **HDR is set to Auto** or ON.

2. **How to Hold the Phone:**
   - Hold the phone naturally with two hands at **chest height** (about 1.2m to 1.4m from the floor).
   - Stand still for half a second before tapping the shutter to avoid motion blur.
   - Look straight through the screen as if you were inspecting the building during your daily routine.

3. **Frame the Scene Naturally:**
   - Do NOT zoom in so tight that only the red cylinder fills the screen.
   - Include the surrounding wall, bracket, and floor line. The AI needs to see *where* the extinguisher is installed relative to the floor.

4. **Vary Your Location:**
   - Walk between 2 to 3 different floors or buildings if possible.
   - Photograph extinguishers against different wall colors (white, beige, brick, concrete).

## 3. Distance Examples

To make sure the model detects extinguishers whether an inspector is standing right in front of it or walking toward it down a long hallway, capture across three distinct distance bands:

```
[Inspector with Phone]
         │
         ├─── 1. CLOSE RANGE (1.0m to 1.5m):
         │    • Stand about 2 to 3 steps away from the wall.
         │    • The extinguisher occupies roughly 40% to 60% of the photo height.
         │    • Pressure gauge, hose, and inspection tag are clearly visible.
         │
         ├─── 2. MEDIUM RANGE (2.5m to 3.5m):
         │    • Stand about 5 to 7 steps away.
         │    • The extinguisher occupies roughly 20% to 35% of the photo height.
         │    • Both the ceiling line and the floor base are visible in the frame.
         │    • This is the primary pose during an active walking safety audit.
         │
         └─── 3. FAR RANGE (5.0m to 7.0m):
              • Stand 10 to 15 steps down the corridor.
              • The extinguisher appears as a smaller red marker (8% to 15% of frame).
              • Teaches the AI to spot life-safety equipment early down a hallway.
```

## 4. Angle Examples

Do not take all photos standing directly in front of the extinguisher. Rotate your viewpoint:

1. **Direct Frontal (0°):**
   - Stand perpendicular to the wall.
   - The cylinder body and front label face directly into the camera.

2. **Oblique Corridor View (30° to 45°):**
   - Stand to the left or right of the extinguisher.
   - Simulates walking down a hallway and viewing the asset from an incoming angle.
   - Captures the side curvature of the cylinder and mounting bracket depth.

3. **Downward Inspection Pitch (15° to 25° Tilt):**
   - Hold the phone at chest level and tilt the lens slightly downward toward the floor.
   - The floor line will appear in the lower half of the frame.
   - This angle is crucial for the spatial rules engine to calculate distance to the floor plane.

## 5. Lighting Examples

Indoor facilities have varied lighting. Actively seek out:

- **Standard Fluorescent / LED:** Normal, evenly lit offices and classroom corridors.
- **Dim / Shadowed:** Stairwells, basements, or end-of-hallway zones where lights are spaced farther apart.
- **Natural Window Daylight / Glare:** Hallways with windows where sunlight casts bright patches or reflections on the wall or glass cabinet doors.

> [!TIP]
> Do NOT use camera flash. Flash creates artificial bright white specular reflections on red paint that do not appear during live video inspection.

## 6. Negative-Image Instructions

Negative images teach the neural network what an **unobstructed corridor** looks like.

### What TO include in Negative Images:
- Clear hallway floors (tiles, linoleum, concrete, carpet).
- Normal baseboards, walls, open and closed doors.
- Ordinary wall items that are **NOT** fire extinguishers (notice boards, light switches, thermostats, door handles).

### What NOT to include in Negative Images:
- Do **NOT** have a fire extinguisher anywhere in the frame (not even tiny in the far background).
- Do **NOT** photograph a blank ceiling or your own feet. The photo must look like a normal hallway scene viewed at eye/chest level.

## 8. Privacy & Ethics Instructions

Workplace and campus data collection must respect privacy:

1. **Avoid Faces:** Do not photograph people's faces. If colleagues or students are walking down the corridor, wait 5 seconds for them to pass or point the camera slightly away.
2. **Avoid Private Information:** Do not photograph open computer monitors, ID badges, whiteboards with passwords/names, or confidential notices.
3. **Authorized Areas Only:** Only photograph public or standard shared areas (hallways, lobbies, stairwells, common rooms). Do not enter restricted or private offices without permission.

## 10. Next Phase — Annotation Protocol (Preview)

Once the 50 images are captured and placed into `data/original_capture/`:

1. **Image Inspection & Review:** We will run a script to verify resolution and file decodability.
2. **Bounding Box Labeling:**

     - **Box Boundary:** Include the red cylinder, the top handle/trigger, the discharge hose/nozzle, and the pressure gauge.

4. **Validation Gatekeeper:** `scripts/validate_dataset.py` will be re-run to confirm `STATUS: PASS` across all 5 classes before initiating YOLO11n training.
